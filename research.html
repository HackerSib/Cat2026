What Is the Gig Economy?

The gig economy is a labour market built on short-term, flexible, project-based work instead of traditional full-time employment. Workers complete individual tasks, or “gigs”, such as ride-hailing, food delivery, web design or consulting, and are connected to customers through digital platforms and mobile apps [1].

How does it work?

It works like a free market: platforms such as Uber, TaskRabbit, Upwork and Airbnb connect customers who need a short-term service directly with independent workers. This has lowered the barriers to entry and turned the “side hustle” into a huge global industry [1].

Advantages for Workers

According to Western Governors University, the gig economy gives workers [2]:

  • Flexibility: set your own hours, choose your days and work remotely, which helps with personal needs such as childcare.
  • Greater independence: manage your own time without direct supervision, supporting work-life balance and job satisfaction.
  • Variety of work: different projects and clients boost creativity and motivation.
  • Earning potential: set your own rates, choose your workload and use tools such as AI to work more efficiently.

These advantages positively affect workers’ lives by giving them control over their time, saving money and time on commuting, and allowing them to study further while building skills [2]. People also stay in gig work because of this independence and because they can earn from several clients at once [4].

Advantages for businesses: lower costs (no benefits or office space), the ability to scale quickly and access to skilled talent on demand [2].

Disadvantages for Workers

  • No traditional benefits: no health insurance, retirement plan or paid leave; workers must arrange their own [2].
  • Taxes and work expenses: workers calculate their own taxes and pay for equipment, software and transport [2].
  • Isolation: working alone can cause a feeling of disconnection from colleagues [2].
  • Stress and unstable income: income changes from month to month and workers must keep finding new gigs [2].

Which disadvantages stop people participating the most?

Anna Ginès of Esade identifies the biggest barriers [3]:

  1. Disguised subordination: platforms control time, pricing and performance, even though workers are called “independent”.
  2. Costs and risk: workers carry the operating costs and supply their own equipment.
  3. No labour protection: self-employed workers are excluded from many rights given to employees.
  4. Precarious conditions: “zero hours” contracts and pay per task make hours and earnings unpredictable.
  5. Difficulty organising: workers are spread out and replaced often, so it is hard to form unions or act together.

Gig Work Compared with Permanent Employment

Woodcock and Graham argue that gig work shifts risk, costs and uncertainty from companies to individual workers [6].

Who Is Affected the Most in South Africa?

Research by Van Belle and colleagues shows that platform work affects the following groups most [7]:

  • Youth (15–24): youth unemployment reached 62.1% in early 2023, so platforms are often the only way in to the labour market.
  • Black workers: poverty and inequality push many into low-paid platform work, often with long, costly commutes.
  • Women: especially in domestic cleaning, where unpaid travel, unsafe conditions and low pay are risks.
  • Migrant workers: barriers to formal jobs lead to over-representation in ride-hailing and delivery.
Our survey fits this picture: 18 of the 20 respondents were aged 15–20, and 55% had never heard of the gig economy.

What if the gig economy did not exist?

Job seekers in South Africa’s high-unemployment market would lose a flexible alternative to traditional work, but everyone would remain fully protected by labour laws such as the Basic Conditions of Employment Act, and would avoid risks like client payment defaults [5].

Conclusion: Answering the Focus Question

Main advantages: flexibility, independence, variety and control over earnings.
Main disadvantages: no benefits, unstable income, stress, weak legal protection and costs carried by the worker.

Gig work suits people who value freedom or need extra income, and it gives young job seekers a way to start earning. However, the risks fall on the worker. Both the research and the survey (50% say the disadvantages outweigh the advantages, 40% say they are equal) suggest that the disadvantages carry slightly more weight, especially for people who depend on gig work as their only income.
