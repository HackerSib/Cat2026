The Gig Economy

Is working “gig by gig” a smart opportunity or a risky trap? This website investigates the gig economy from a worker’s point of view.

Focus Question

What are the main advantages and disadvantages of participating in the gig economy for workers?

Focus Area

The worker’s experience of gig work, looking at:

  • flexibility and independence
  • income opportunities and stability
  • job security and legal protection
  • working conditions compared with permanent jobs

Abstract

The gig economy is a labour market in which people earn money from short-term, flexible jobs found through digital platforms such as Uber, TaskRabbit and Upwork, instead of holding a permanent job. It is growing quickly, but it is not clear whether it truly benefits the people who do the work.

To answer the focus question, ten research questions were investigated using reliable websites, a book and an academic article. The findings were summarised in a planning document. A questionnaire was also completed by 20 respondents; their answers were captured in a spreadsheet and a database, analysed and turned into graphs.

The research shows that the main advantages are flexible hours, independence, variety and control over earnings. The main disadvantages are no employee benefits, unstable income, stress, weak legal protection and costs paid by the worker. In South Africa, young people are affected the most. In the survey, flexible hours were chosen most often as the biggest benefit (40%), while stress and uncertainty was the biggest problem (35%). Sixty percent of respondents would lean towards a permanent job. Overall, the disadvantages carry slightly more weight than the advantages, although gig work remains a valuable option for people who cannot find permanent employment.
